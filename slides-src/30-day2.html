The most common dashboard is a wall: every widget the builder found, arranged by when they found it. Nobody can read a wall.

The blueprint

Five questions earn five places.

#Priya's questionWidget that answers it
Q1What are both teams working on, and is the sprint on track?Chart by Feature and State · Burndown
Q2What have we delivered, and is delivery steady?Velocity, one per team
Q3Are there urgent bugs, and are they growing?Query tile + trend
Q4How are Features progressing toward their dates?Features by State · at-risk list
Q5What's at risk or blocked, and is effort going where it should?Risk, blocked and orphan tiles
The removal testIf you can't say which question a widget answers, it's decoration. When the dashboard grows, anything that serves none of the five comes off.

Constraint for participants: at least one question about Features, and at least one that looks forward to next sprint. CFD and Cycle Time are diagnostics that support Q1 and Q2.

Thirteen widgets, one page

A cockpit for the people who run delivery

  • A Markdown header: owner, last review, what each color means.
  • Count + list side by side: the tile starts a conversation, the list says which one.
  • Analytics widgets read exactly one team. Set Team first; put its name in the title.
  • A threshold is a policy: decide what turns a tile red, and write it down.

Reef: the right query on the wrong page. A reused query brings its own scope; check it against the page title.

Cut it down for executives

Cockpit for managers. Windscreen for execs.

13 → 6widgets. Velocity, burndown, CFD and cycle time stay on the manager's page.
The most important widgetis words: a bottom line and the decision needed. It's the only place you say what the numbers mean.
The 30-second testShow the room the exec view for 30 seconds, then hide it. Can they say the bottom line and the decision being asked?

Velocity side by side on an exec page invites the cross-team comparison you warned against yesterday.

Make the gut call before you see the evidence. Write it down.

Priya, 9:12 AM: "Someone saw 44 against 26 and asked if Grace's team needs support. Diego tells me Mobile is drowning. I'd rather not guess."

Writing the judgment down first is the only honest way to learn which numbers fool you. Most rooms split; some pick B because of "lower throughput".

Lighthouse insight

One chart is an anecdote. Three make a case.

Exhibit A

Output

Velocity, throughput (count), commitment reliability.

Exhibit B

Flow

Cumulative flow, cycle time, work in progress.

Exhibit C

Outcomes

Bugs left behind, work that reached a Feature.

The ruleAct when output, flow and outcomes agree. When they disagree, take the question to the team.

Never reach a verdict on one chart.

Exhibit A · Output

Points up a third. Stories finished: flat.

Mobile velocity (points)

Mobile stories completed (count)

Velocity measures estimates, not speedAverage points per story went 2.75 → 3.67. Same work, bigger numbers. Throughput (count) measures finishing.

Velocity is a team's sum of its own estimates. Rising points over a flat count is inflation. Screenshot available: lab06-velocity-points-vs-count.

Exhibit B · Flow

The Resolved band is widening.

Mobile cycle time, Activated → Closed (days)

Digital Accounts holds steady at 4–5 days.

Little's Law, in manager wordsMore work in progress means slower, not faster. The queue forms in front of one tester.

The CFD shows where work waits; cycle time shows what the wait costs.

Exhibit C, then the verdict

Mobile Banking needs help. Not a target.

9 → 42open bugs in five sprints
52%of Sprint 13 points served no Feature

Digital Accounts' lower throughput is a capacity fact to plan around, not a problem to fix.

The help plan

  • Limit WIP; stop mid-sprint intake
  • Add or swarm on test capacity
  • Reserve capacity for bugs; agree a quality bar
  • Each action: an owner and a metric
Goodhart's lawWhen a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure. Never set a goal a team can hit by changing what it types.

Metrics are conversation starters. "Help" never means a velocity target.

Translate before you write

Executives don't think in Story Points.

42 open bugs, 3 Critical, 4 at P1

Members are finding defects in production, including a deposit failure nobody owns yet.

Fraud Alerts 4% done

Every sprint without it, members learn about fraud from their statement, not their phone.

71% of plan delivered

Every Mobile date is about 30% optimistic.

About 45% of Mobile bugs since Sprint 11 are tagged escaped-defect.

Make a call

"Zero of 13 committed Features closed." Red?

ARed: nothing has shipped
BAmber: worth watching
CNeither, on its own. It needs a baseline.

It's week 2 of PI 4's second sprint, and Features close near their Target Dates. Only Check Deposit is due now, and it won't close. That's the red.

Reef: a number with no baselineGive every executive number a comparison: against plan, against last month, or against a threshold.

Ask the room to vote before revealing. A tile like this with a red rule would cause a false alarm every PI.

BLUF

Answer first. Ask second. Evidence after.

  1. Bottom line: will we make it?
  2. Decisions needed: each with one named person
  3. Top risks: the two, in members' words
  4. Evidence: every number one click away
  5. Options considered
  6. What happens next

An executive should be able to stop after two sentences and still know what to do. Every number on the dashboard sits next to a threshold or a baseline.

Under pressure from Dana's question cards, answer the same way: answer, evidence, decision.

A model answer · the BLUF panel at the top of the exec dashboard

Two sentences. Two decisions. Two names.

METP Executive Status · Updated: Sprint 14, week 2

Bottom line: Fraud Alerts (priority 1) misses PI 4 unless Simone makes one trade this week. Mobile Banking needs two sprints of stabilization.

Decisions needed: (1) Simone: API into Sprint 15, or Fraud Alerts to Sprint 17. (2) Dana: no mid-sprint intake for Mobile, plus temporary test capacity.

Thresholds sit underneath: at-risk Features green at 3 or fewer · Mobile P1 bugs green at 0 · PI 4 closed read against Target Dates.

Dana can stop reading after the first sentence and still know what to do. Everything else on the dashboard is evidence for these lines.

Two reviews, thirty minutes each

A briefing is a snapshot. A review keeps it true.

Weekly delivery review

"What changed, what's stuck?"

You, Ruth, the Product Owners. Unblock and re-plan inside the PI. Every red item leaves with an owner.

Monthly steering review

"Still the right things?"

Dana and the executives. Decide trade-offs between priorities, update the Epic rank, record decisions.

The rule that would have saved Lab 7If a date moved in Azure DevOps, the dependent dates move in the same meeting.

A stale dashboard is worse than none, because people trust it. If the BLUF panel still says Sprint 14 in Sprint 16, it's telling the committee something false in your name.
