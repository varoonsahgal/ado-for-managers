## Summary

New members increasingly start online, but many do not finish there. The biggest single drop-off is **ID verification**. Members who are onboarded in a branch hold more products, yet most older branch-onboarded members never start using digital banking. Members who switch channels mid-application are asked to start over. Onboarding works for members who stay in one channel and fails the ones who move between them.

### 1. Most members start online

A majority of new members (58%) begin their application on the web or in the app. Online is now the front door, even for members who end up finishing elsewhere.

### 2. ID verification is where online applications die

41% of online applications are abandoned at the ID verification step. Members described repeated photo retakes, unclear error messages and no way to get help without starting over.

### 3. Branch onboarding is slow

A branch onboarding takes 47 minutes on average. Much of that time is re-keying information and printing and signing forms.

### 4. Branch onboarding builds deeper relationships

Members onboarded in a branch hold 2.1 products after 12 months, compared with 1.4 for digital-only members. The conversation with a person matters.

### 5. Older branch members stay out of digital

62% of branch-onboarded members over 60 never activate digital banking. They are not offered help to get started, and many are worried about fraud.

### 6. Switching channels means starting over

When a member starts online and then visits a branch, staff re-enter the member's data 83% of the time. The branch systems cannot see the online application.

## Method notes

- **Application funnel analytics:** online application events from start to account opened, by step. Abandonment is counted at the last step a member reached.
- **Branch time study:** branch staff timed new-member onboarding sessions from greeting to account opened, across a sample of branches.
- **Product holdings analysis:** products held 12 months after onboarding, compared by onboarding channel.
- **Digital activation:** whether a member logged in to online or mobile banking at least once after onboarding, by age band.
- **Channel switching:** applications started online and completed in a branch, checked for re-entered data.
- **Member interviews:** in-branch and remote interviews with recently onboarded members and with applicants who abandoned. Quotes are anonymized and lightly edited for length.

Limitations: the branch time study covers a sample of branches, not all 74. Interview participants self-selected, so quotes illustrate findings rather than measure them.

## Implications for Project Lighthouse

- Fix ID verification first. It is the largest, most measurable loss in the online funnel.
- Let members save and resume, and carry an online application into a branch without re-entry.
- Shorten branch onboarding by removing re-keying, not by removing the conversation.
- Offer older branch members a guided, person-led start to digital banking, with fraud reassurance.

## Open questions

- How many abandoning applicants come back later through another channel?
- Which ID document types fail verification most often?
- What would persuade members over 60 to try digital banking?
